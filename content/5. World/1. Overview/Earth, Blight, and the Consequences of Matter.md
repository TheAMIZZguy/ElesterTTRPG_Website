#  The Matter Classes and Conscious Fields

In Elester, matter responds to consciousness through fields (alchemy), not just through chemistry. The field of Existence governs the inanimate, and splits into two opposed manifestations:

- **Earth** – the stable, constructive shaping of matter.
- **Blight** – the corrosive, entropic un-shaping of matter.

Although chemical understanding of the world is quite limited to necessities like farming and herbal remedies. Alchemical understanding has been well developed in viewing the different manifolds that objects take up and have been classed by similarities in their own manifold manifestation. From this, all inanimate matter is grouped into six Matter Classes, based on *how it “is”* in the world:

1. **Metals** – dense, conductive, structural metals and alloys
2. **Gases & Particulate** – air, fumes, smoke, dust, vapour
3. **Liquids** – fluid substances that flow and pool (water, oils, solvents, etc.)
4. **Organics (dead)** – flesh, wood, plant matter, bone, leather, dead biological material
5. **Earthy** – soil, clay, sand, mud, moss,  decomposed organic mush
6. **Rocky** – rocks, minerals, crystals, gravel, gemstones

These classes are *consciousness categories*: the Fields “see” a chunk of iron more like other metals than like stone, even if a human might classify both as “hard stuff.” And as such, the manifolds relating to matter are vastly different between classes, leading to the classification and specialty of many Existence maxems. 

It should be noted that not everything has been discovered, and the above classifications shadow the "default" understanding of the world. Something like mercury would be classified under liquid and is controllable via liquid flow manifolds; though transmutation and creation efforts have shown little progress. In a similar manner, molten metals cannot usually be created via liquid manifolds unless inside the heart of a volcano: as the classes in that environment actually change from what is seen above. But this is an aside, the comments in this paragraph show the real nature of matter, but this is largely unknown or are considered myths by the residents of Elester.

- **Earth maxems** learn to create, refine, and stabilize matter in these classes.
- **Blight maxems** learn to corrode, poison, rot, and disintegrate those same classes.

> **Important design note (mixed objects):**  
> Many real items are composites (e.g. an axe = metal head + wooden haft; a home = stone + timber + nails).  
> Creating or destroying specific objects usually requires:
> - multiple specializations (e.g. Metal + Organics)
> - specific feats that allow “mixed-class” manipulation
> - weaves that allow "shaped" manipulation

# Earth Creation by Rank

Earth manifolds focus on creating or refining matter in one of the six classes. Higher ranks mean:

- better purity or quality,
- more complex or desirable materials
- ~~and more ability to stabilize matter (make it last longer / permanently).~~

You can map ranks onto proficiency or feat progression however you like. A simple option:

- **Trained** → Rank 1  
- **Skilled** → Ranks 1–2  
- **Mastered** → Ranks 1–3  
- **Legendary** → Ranks 1–5 (with Rank 5 gated by additional feats)

> **Note on permanence:**  
> By default, created matter wants to fade. Anything lasting more than a few seconds/minutes starts to cost a lot more MP. And as such, any consumed created matter do not provide long-term nutrition.

# Blight Destruction by Rank

Blight manifolds work on the same six classes, but invert their role:

- Blight can:
  - directly inflict damage,
  - weaken or curse materials,
  - or break the *capacity* of a place to support life or hold structure.

As with Earth, you can tie ranks to proficiency:

- **Trained** → Rank 1  
- **Skilled** → Ranks 1–2  
- **Mastered** → Ranks 1–3  
- **Legendary** → Ranks 1–5  (with Rank 5 gated by additional feats)

> Disintegrating or cursing a **composite object** (fortress, warship, enchanted weapon) often needs:
> - separate **Blight ranks** in multiple classes, **or**

## Earth vs Blight (Quick Contrast)

**Earth**:
  - Creates, stabilizes, refines, and supports structure and industry.
  - Focus: building, supplying, repairing, fortifying.

**Blight**:
  - Corrodes, poisons, rots, and unravels the same classes of matter.
  - Focus: sabotage, attrition, area denial, curses, plagues.

# Economic and Cultural Consequences (Brief Notes)

These systems have big implications for the setting (even if players mostly care about what they can evoke in combat):

**Trade & Currency**
  - Earth can make temporary precious materials, and it is costly to evoke them to be shaped
  - Long-lasting gold/gems are still extremely costly to conjure, so mining and minting remain core industries  
  - Fear of conjured counterfeits pushes many states toward guild-stamped coinage, bank-backed vouchers, and proto–fiat currency
  - There are experts that can distinguish between natural and created gold

**Warfare**
  - Earth maxems reshape battlefields (walls, trenches, fortifications, supply materials)
  - Blight maxems degrade equipment (corroded armor, sabotaged fortresses), poison supply lines, and create blight-zones that constrain movement
  - High-rank Blight (gases, plagues) is often outlawed or tightly regulated, seen as magical WMDs

**Agriculture**
  - Earth (Earthy/Organics) boosts farming: fertile soils, irrigation channels, storage structures
  - Blight can ruin harvests, salt the earth, creating damage long after they're gone

**Craft, Fashion & Ritual**
  - Earth enables ephemeral luxury: conjured jewels, silk, and gold that last long enough for a festival or audience
  - This creates slang and social dynamics:
    - “Fading gold” – someone who looks rich but is overextended or lying
    - “Conjured crown” – a ruler or official who appears powerful but has weak real backing
  - Temples use ephemeral offering (conjured gold that fades on the altar) to emphasize intention over material wealth
