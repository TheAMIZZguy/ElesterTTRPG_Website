Ability Scores represent your characters natural capacities and talents, like the potential you have when you are born. But it is skills that show what your character finds important enough to train and allows you to stand out. Just like you can be smart and be a doctor and know nothing of mathematics or be strong but unable to climb up a rockwall. They represent your characters expertise in a skill and may allow you to do things beyond the standard.


Each skill is tied to a core ability; when rolling for a specific skill, aside from your proficiency in said skill, you would also add the related core ability modifier. Such as adding charisma to your Persuasion check. Note that the ability modifier may be changed at the GMs discretion for specific scenarios, a famous example of which is using Strength over Charisma for Persuasion rolls in the case of trying to intimidate the other person to persuade them to a certain course of action. 

## Improving Skills
There are four (three?) standard ways for your character to increase their skills as they progress through a campaign. As your character levels up, there will be opportunities to increase your proficiencies in skills you already have trained. Or additionally as you increase your ability scores to get new skills. There are also certain feats (idk if this last one will stay) that contain the skill (todo link) property that will often grant you new ways of using a skill. And the final one, at GM discretion, is from simple downtime training and development if the game you're playing has the downtime to allow it.  

Other ways of increasing your skill modifiers and proficiency are more temporary, such as a specific item or effect that increases your score, or at the GM discretion, as a reward for completing certain tasks. 

## Skill Actions
There are five tiers of proficiency for a skill: Untrained, Trained, Skilled, Mastered, Legendary, and aside from the bonuses to rolls they may grant; certain abilities may require you to be at a certain tier of proficiency to use it. In addition, anyone can use a skill's untrained actions, or those they have already surpassed as a tier. Specific circumstances or conditions may cause the required proficiency for a skill to increase or decrease. An example could be; a character Proficient in Engineering (Alchemy) would be generally be able to identify basic potions (TODO check that this still happens) might unable to identify a basic potion if said potion is the first of its kind in a setting (but may be able to decipher what it would likely do if they had Mastered the skill). The GM
decides if such changes happen.

The actions of a skill aren’t comprehensive; the GM might ask you to attempt a skill check without using any of the listed actions, or times when the GM asks you to roll using a different core ability modifier (recall your level of proficiency is related to the skill, not the ability modifier). Most skills include entries for Success and Failure, as well as descriptions of what occurs on a Critical Success or a Critical Failure. If either of the critical entries is absent, treat those results as a success or failure, as normal.

Actions will often have related improvements as you gain higher levels of proficiency, such as Recall Knowledge: at untrained you can recall the name of a key noble or ruler, and at legendary you can remember the details of a long-lost religion.

Additionally, there are General Skills, which are skills that can usually be done with several skills in most situations. And other traits that are skill-specific but are not actions. All such actions and traits are described below.

## Skill Checks and Skill DCs
In the situations where you actively need to use a skill, you will often need to perform a skill check: rolling a d20 and adding the relevant modifiers.

**Skill Check = d20 + Core Ability Bonus + Skill Proficiency Bonus + (Item + Situational + Status Bonuses/Penalties)**

Remember that you can only benefit from one type of bonus once, but penalties are cumulative

The score you are trying to reach is set by the GM based on the difficulty of the task, but a guideline is as follows (todo tweak as needed)

There are four separate Degrees of Success/Failure for a skill check, you must select the highest choice you fall under after a roll

| Degree of Success | Score                                |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Critical Success  | *Match or exceed* the DC + 10        |
| Success           | *Match or exceed* the DC             |
| Failure           | *Exceed* the DC - 10                 |
| Critical Failure  | *Match or do worse* than the DC - 10 |
i.e. if the DC is 13 and your skill check ended up with 23 or higher, you would have a critical success, 13 to 22 is a success, 4 to 12 is a failure, and 3 or less is a critical failure.

Now, after the roll and Degree of Success is known, you also consider the roll of the d20, on a *Nat 20* you automatically go up a tier of success (up to Critical Success), and on a *Nat 1* you go down a tier of success (down to a Critical Failure). Some Feats or other effects may change the dice rolls needed to move your tier, such as going up a tier on a Nat 19 *or* Nat 20. The result of this is the **Rolled Degree of Success** (i.e. a *Rolled Success* or *Rolled Critical Failure*).

After this, other features or circumstances may come into effect that modify your tier of success. Such as treating any Rolled Success as a Critical Success for a specific skill. Some of these may be positive or negative. If you have multiple such modifiers that may benefit your degree of success, you may only choose *one* (i.e. you cannot have a Rolled Failure bumped into a Success using one Feat, and then turn that Success into a Critical Success with a second Feat). Likewise, with any such modifiers that harm your Degree of Success, only one may effect you, but it must be the one that will bump you down to the lowest Degree of Success possible. 

For scenarios where you have something that benefits your Degree of Success and something that harms it, you apply the following: 
- A benefit/harm that would increase you past a Critical Success/Failure is still considered for the purposes of this effect
	- i.e. If your Rolled Degree of Success was a Critical Success, and you have a feature that would increase any Rolled Degrees of Success as a degree higher still applies to negate a harm that would treat a Rolled Critical Success as a Success
- If the benefit cannot effect the Rolled Degree of Success, then only the harm is considered
	- i.e. A feat that treats a Rolled Success as a Critical Success does not apply to negate a harm if the Rolled Degree of Success was a Failure or Critical Failure
- If the harm cannot effect the Rolled Degree of Success, then only the benefit is considered
	- i.e. A feat that treats a Rolled Failure as a Critical Failure does not apply to negate a harm if the Rolled Degree of Success was a Success or Critical Success
- If both a harm and a benefit would effect the roll (and only one of each needs to exist), then no modifications are applied past the Rolled Degree of Success, even if more could apply.

## Notable Skill Check Properties

#### Secret
If an action has the Secret property, then a player may not not whether they success at a Skill Check. The GM would roll the check for said player and inform them of the effects without revealing the results. This is often done when knowledge about an outcome is imperfect, such as looking for a trap or translating a message. The GM may choose to ignore this property, but should note that players may subconsciously act differently if they knew the result. 

#### Unique
If an action has the Unique property then it means that the party must all be convinced of the same result. This will usually happen in two ways: one person rolls for the entire party (if possible, they may still get help bonuses) and the result is shared with the party, or everyone that can roll then rolls, and the party chooses the most common result (at GM discretion in the case of a tie). The GM may choose how this property is implemented on a case-to-case basis and may even ignore this property, but should note that this may lead to an excess of rolls and successes for a situation. 

#### Exploration and Downtime
Exploration activities usually take a minute or more, while Downtime activities may take a day or more. As the names suggest, these are generally used for when the party is in Exploration or Downtime. Additionally, this means that these actions cannot be done during an encounter, but specific situations at GM discretion may alter the specific timings (i.e. a grand ritual that usually takes hours is nearly complete and was abandoned may only need a minute when the party stumbles upon it, and thus may be done in Exploration).
