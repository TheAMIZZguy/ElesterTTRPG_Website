# Maxem Rules — Out of Combat

The out-of-combat reference for Maxems: how the Field behaves outside a fight, how manifolds scale to longer tasks, what exploration and downtime unlock, and where your element changes the world rather than the initiative order.

Most of what a Maxem does in the world is not combat. The Field doesn't care about turns.


## The Field Outside Combat

In combat, evocation is a precise act — declared, costed, resolved. Outside combat, the Field is less adversarial. It doesn't resist you the same way when nothing is trying to kill you.

The rules for MP cost, Complexity, and anchors all still apply. What changes is the pace. You're not competing with a reaction stack. You can take your time, prepare your anchors properly, and attempt things that would be too slow or too risky to try mid-fight.

Many manifolds that would require Duration or Sustain weaves in combat can be held naturally outside it — the Field cooperates with sustained intent when nothing is disrupting it. The GM decides when a long-duration out-of-combat evocation requires you to actively maintain it versus when it simply persists.

## Manifold Duration Out of Combat

Most manifolds are instant by default. Out of combat, the Duration weave's tier ladder still applies, but the cost structure changes slightly.

You pay the MP cost once to establish the effect. If you want it to last longer than the manifold's natural duration, you pay the Sustain cost at the intervals defined by your Duration tier. You don't need to spend AP to sustain out of combat — the cost is MP and your conscious attention.

If you stop paying attention — sleep, unconsciousness, being in a different building — an effect that requires your focus ends. An effect that doesn't require focus (a frozen block of ice, a created object, a lit flame) persists on its own until the manifold's end condition is met.

## Evocation Time Out of Combat

Outside combat, you're not counting AP. You're counting time.

As a loose baseline:

- A manifold you could cast at 1–2 AP in combat takes a few seconds out of combat.
- A manifold at 3–4 AP takes roughly half a minute.
- A manifold requiring multiple rounds (Weave Adept territory) takes several minutes.
- A ritual-scale evocation — extremely high Complexity, rare materials, significant MP — takes an hour or more.

The GM may ask you to commit to an uninterrupted time window for longer evocations. If something disrupts you mid-cast, you lose the MP spent and start over.

## Civic and Practical Use

A Maxem is not only useful in danger. Most of the world sees a Maxem not as a weapon but as a specialist — someone who solves problems that would otherwise take a great deal of time, equipment, or labour.

What this looks like varies by element:

**Fire**: controlled heat for forges, kilns, and ovens; warming buildings in winter; burning cleared land; signalling.
**Ice**: preserving food and medicine for long journeys; cooling during heatstroke or fever; making ice for wounds; keeping water drinkable.

**Life**: accelerating growth of crops and timber; stabilizing wounds before a surgeon arrives; treating infections and parasites; keeping pack animals healthy.
**Death**: safely breaking down biological waste; quarantine work; identifying cause of death; controlled accelerated decay of dangerous organic material.

**Earth**: reinforcing construction; detecting structural weakness; sourcing and refining materials; creating tools on the road.
**Blight**: controlled demolition; neutralizing contaminated soil or water at low ranks; sabotage; controlled rot of stubborn organic matter.

**Flow**: irrigation; favourable travel conditions on water; lifting and moving heavy loads; ventilating enclosed spaces.
**Static**: illumination; sending signals; demagnetizing equipment; building illusions for theatre, diplomacy, or misdirection.

None of this is exhaustive. The GM and player should use the element's identity as the guide — if it fits the Field, it's probably possible at the right Complexity and rank.


## Reading the Field

Outside combat, a Maxem's relationship to the Field becomes subtly perceptive. You don't just evoke through it — you notice it.

With Field Sense, you can spend a few moments and an Aetherics check to read residue in your element at your location — who used it, roughly how recently, and at roughly what intensity. The Residue Analyst feat deepens this into identifying manifold types, weave signatures, and the rough Complexity band of what was evoked.

Out of combat, this is an investigative tool. You're not reading live evocations — you're reading echoes. A skilled Maxem walking into a room where someone recently evoked Fire might notice warmth that has no source, or a faint scent of char. These are environmental tells, not mechanical detections, and the GM should describe them as such before asking for a check.

## Detecting Other Maxems

If another Maxem is actively evoking within range of your Field Sense, you may notice it without a check — the Field moves. What you detect is approximate: the direction, the element if it's the same as yours or the opposite, and a rough sense of intensity.

If the other Maxem has [[Subtle Tint]], the detection DC increases. If they are using [[Thoughtcasting]] or otherwise evoking with minimal signature, you may only notice the residue after the fact.

You cannot read another element's active evocation with Field Sense alone. You notice the disturbance in the Field without knowing its nature. A Vasilian can identify what you cannot.


## Exploration

The exploration turn is the unit of time in which the party moves through and interacts with an environment. A Maxem has specific things they can do during exploration that other archetypes cannot.

Intended exploration actions for Maxems:

- Detect Residue: spend an exploration turn to read the recent aether history of a space. Useful for knowing whether a dungeon room has seen recent magical activity, or whether a trade route is being watched.
- Sustain an Effect: actively maintain a manifold across an exploration turn without it lapsing — useful for held illumination, sustained terrain modification, or active concealment fields.
- Prepare Ground: spend an exploration turn modifying terrain or environment in ways that will matter in the next encounter — creating chokepoints, icing a corridor, blighting a water source, creating a light source that will persist.
- Read the Environment: use your element to gather information about a space that would take other archetypes significant skill investment to obtain — a Fire Maxem reading heat signatures, an Earth Maxem assessing structural integrity, a Static Maxem detecting illusions.

These are not formalized yet. They are intended to give Maxems meaningful choices during exploration that are distinct from what they do in combat.


## Downtime

*(TODO: This section is a placeholder. Full downtime rules are a separate development track. The notes below are intentions, not finished mechanics.)*

Downtime is the period between adventures — days or weeks in a settlement, recovering, preparing, and pursuing personal projects. A Maxem's downtime use of the Field tends toward craft, investigation, and community function.

Intended downtime activities for Maxems:

- Civic Work: provide a sustained service to a settlement — heating a bathhouse, treating a contaminated well, illuminating a public space, preserving a season's harvest. The scale of what you can do is gated by your Complexity maximum and the time you're willing to spend. This generates reputation, income, or faction standing depending on the context.
- Research: study an unfamiliar manifold through practice and observation. Out of combat you can attempt to learn manifolds you've witnessed in the wild, not just those you've been taught — this is slower and riskier than feat-gated learning, but possible.
- Craft Assistance: work alongside an Artificer or Alchemist whose materials or processes your element can enhance. A Fire Maxem assists a forge; a Life Maxem assists a chirgeon's preparation; a Static Maxem assists a jeweler working with crystals. This produces better outputs than either specialist could achieve alone.
- Recovery: a Maxem who spent heavily in a previous encounter may use downtime to restore VP and address Hexed conditions that wouldn't fully clear on a standard rest.
- Investigation: pursue the residue of a crime, a disappearance, or a historical event through careful field-reading over multiple days. This is the slow version of the exploration detection actions — more thorough, more revealing, but requiring uninterrupted access to the site.


## Manifold Learning Outside Combat

By default, you learn manifolds through feats. Outside combat, there is a second path: exposure and practice.

If you witness a manifold of your element being evoked — by an enemy, an ally, or an NPC — and you can identify what it was (via Residue Analyst or a successful Aetherics check), you may attempt to learn it during downtime through practice.

This takes time measured in days, not sessions. The GM sets the DC based on the manifold's Complexity and how complete your understanding of the evocation is. A partial observation gives you a harder DC; observing it twice, or studying the residue carefully, brings it down.

This is intentionally not a shortcut past the feat system. It is a supplement — one that rewards attentive players and creates a reason to care about what enemies are doing, not just whether they're a threat.

## The Subtle Tint of the Field

Over time, a Maxem's relationship to their element changes their perception of the world. This is not a mechanical rule — it's a framing principle for roleplay and GM description.

A Fire Maxem sees where energy is hungry to move. Cold surfaces look like they're holding something back. A lit hearth reads as a natural thing, a candle as effortful. They're not wrong — the Field is genuinely doing something in all of those cases.

An Ice Maxem perceives equilibrium being enforced. Heat is what keeps things from settling. The world is always slightly restless around them, and their element is always trying to calm it.

These perceptions don't grant mechanical bonuses beyond what Field Sense provides. They are how the world looks to someone who has spent years learning to project intent in a way the Field recognizes. The GM should occasionally describe the world through this lens — especially in environments with elevated Field State — so that being a Maxem feels different from not being one.



## Quick Reference

The Field does not turn off. You can evoke outside combat with relaxed anchors, longer timescales, and more room to attempt things that would be impractical mid-fight.

MP and Complexity still apply. Anchors relax by one tier by default. Duration is time rather than rounds. The GM determines when sustained effects require active attention and when they simply persist.

The most important thing a Maxem does outside combat is change the situation before combat begins — or avoid combat entirely by being the person who already solved the problem.
