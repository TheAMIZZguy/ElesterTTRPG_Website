# The Maxem

What in other worlds might call a magician, the residents of Elester call a maxem. 
The name for the rare human that can control aspects of the world that seems ubiquitous due to all the beasts that can control the same aspects. 
It is simply a talent for a science rather than a proper magic as we would call it. 

## What a Maxem Is

A Maxem is a specialist who evokes ("casts") manifolds ("spells") in one Element and sculpts them with weaves (modifiers) until it becomes exactly what the moment demands. 
Some think of it divine blessing, others a skill learned the hard way. 

In play, that means:
- set the tempo by altering terrain, motion, heat, light, or vitality,
- trade components, positioning, and risk to get cleaner outcomes,
- and make the party’s plan _work_ by giving them the conditions they need.

You’re not a walking artillery piece (unless you choose that path). 
You’re someone who can will reality to match what you need; you're at your best when you change the situation, not just deal damage.


## Design Intent

The Maxem fills a role of environmental and situational control.
- **Martials** force outcomes with skill and steel.
- **Vasilians** perfect choices by reading the Fields.
- **Alchemists** use intelligence to give them an edge.
- **Artificers** make sure their gear will outlast yours in combat.
- **Maxems** force outcomes by manipulating a Field.

This class is designed to:
- feel useful in combat and problem-solving scenes,
- reward creative construction (chassis + weaves),
- keep power gated by cost and difficulty,
- and enhance the capabilities of the party by creating conditions.

A party with a Maxem fights on terms they helped write.


## How You Touch the Field

The element you work with doesn't quite feel like you're *controlling* or *forcing* it to act in a certain way, rather it feels like an extension of your body that you weren't born with.

At first it’s clumsy: intention leaks, focus wobbles, output is inconsistent. 
Over time, you learn the _semblance_ of the Element—where it flows, what it resists, what it does “on its own”, and what always costs extra.

The Field doesn’t let one gain mastery of it from the start, control exists because your mind learns how to project intent in a way the Element recognizes, and it eventually begins to grant more. 

That relationship changes perception, you begin to see the subtle tint of the field across everything, fundamentally changing the way you view the world. 
- A Fire maxem sees where energy is hungry to move, while an Ice maxem perceives it as calm being enforced to equilibrium.


## In Combat: Your Usual Loop

Maxem manifolds are built, not selected.


1. **Choose a chassis** (Launch, Buff, Transmute, harm, etc.).
2. **Weave** (damage, duration, shape, economy, safety, etc.).
3. **Evoke** through your Field’s excitation.
4. **Resolve** effects

That’s how “firebolt” becomes “fireball”:  
- same core intent (launch harm in the form of fire), but re-shaped with range + area + power.

The price of customization is that every weave increases cost, and often Level.

One must be careful of how much of their will they excerpt, for that fatigue recovers slowly.


## Mana and Levels

You are not omnipotent, you cannot change everything every second of the world.

**Mana Points**: How much willpower you can exert before needing rest (akin to HP).
- There is a maximum value of this you have. 
- Evoking costs Mana.
- Sustaining effects costs Mana.
**Level**: is difficulty, how hard a manifold is to evoke _correctly_.
- A manifold near your maximum Level often requires **anchors**: staff, gestures, voice—anything that makes your intent _stable_.
- A manifold near your maximum difficulty may require tools like a staff and vocal components to properly focus while evoking.

Evoking Manifolds usually cost Mana, unless you have mastered it to the point that it is effortless/second nature for you.
- Usually requires a mix of high enough level compared to the manifold and feats that increase mastery. 


## Out of Combat

Outside combat, you’re doing the same thing—just more broadly and subtly:
- **Civic work**: irrigation, decontamination, lighting, cooling, etc.
- **Craft & repair**: heating, shaping, transmuting, healing, etc.
- **Travel & logistics**: favourable winds, building ladders and bridges, etc..
- **Intel**: trace aether use, read residues.

Maxems turn "we can't" to "there's a way".


## How Maxem Feat Trees Work

Your feat paths usually deepen one or more:

- **Manifold ladder**: breadth/depth of base effects in your Field.
- **Weave mastery**: geometry, tempo (quicken/hold/trigger/echo/sustain), economy, safety.
- **Ease of use**: from needing tools to help get into the right mindset, to being able to do it with a thought.
- **Counter-work**: counter-evokes and reading residuals.


## Attribute Philosophy

**Primary Attribute:** 
- **Connection (CON)**: how cleanly your evocations take.
**Secondary:** 
- **Knowledge (KNO)**: how many manifold chassis' you know.
- **Wits (WIT)**: how many weaves you know.
- **Presence (PRE)**: how much you can evoke.
- **Charisma (CHA)**: how well you _read_ and interpret aether signatures and residuals (see vasilian).

**Attack vs Defense**: You roll Connection vs a target’s Aetherical or Physical Defense, depending on the feature.

**Key skills (TODO):** Aetherology (what), Aetherics (live read), Perception, Tactics.

**Resource**: Your limiter is Mana and Levels (Difficulty).


## Specialized Archetypes (by Element)

Each Archetype is a focus inside an element of a field. But most difference comes from the creativity one can use their abilities. 

### Fire

**Identity:** Accelerator / breaker.  
You increase energy: ignition, pressure, momentum, heat, radiant flare.

- **Damage** throw fire, beams, explosions.
- **Control**: lines of denial and forced movement via heat
- **Support**: controlled warmth, forging, etc.
- **Buff**: magnify aether effects.

### Ice

**Identity:** Preserver / dampener.
You reduce energy: stasis, slowing, preservation, stability.

- **Control**: slippery ground, pressure-numbing, slow fields.
- **Damage**: throw shards of ice and freeze enemies.
- **Support** : heat-resist, build structures of ice, etc.
- **Debuff**: reduce the energy of the fields.

### Life

**Identity:** Restorer / guide .  
You push vitality and micro-agency: repair, stabilize, enhance function.

- **Heal**: restore, stabilize, reduce shock.
- **Buff**: mental focus and resilience. 
- **Imbue**: micro-agency in items.
- **Terrain**: accelerate natural growth.

### Death

**Identity:** Severer / siphoner.  
You deal in separation and decline: unraveling living patterns, draining momentum.

- **Damage**: necrotic withering, vitality drains, debilitation.
- **Debuff**: fray concentration, worsen tactics.
- **Curse**: long-term weakening.
- **Siphon**: draw object-mana, accelerate breakdown/decay.

### Earth

**Identity:** Shaper / builder.  
You command cohesion and form: structure, weight, reinforcement, transmutation.

- **Control**: create and modify terrain, transmute ally and enemy gear.
- **Elemental**: the many different types of material classes one can specialize in.
- **Support**: detect materials/fakes, forging, etc. 

### Blight

**Identity:** Dissolver / contaminant.  
You master dissolution: corrosion, toxins, rot, degradation of structure.

- **Control**: destroy and rot terrain and gear.
- **Elemental**: the many different types of material classes one can specialize in.
- **Damage**: acids and poisons. 

### Flow

**Identity:** Mover / carrier  
You shape motion: air, water, bodies, gravity.

- **Control**: reposition allies/enemies, redirect projectiles, restrict movement. 
- **Buff**: improve speed and strength.
- **Damage**: impact, compression, thrown debris.

### Static 

**Identity:** Binder / veil-maker.  
You bend light, charge, magnetism, and physics in stasis. 

- **Support**: illusions, darkness, mirage angles, sensory interference.
- **Damage**: shocks and short stuns.
- **Control**: magnetize/demagnetize and move ferrous objects. 

### Magus 

**Identity:** Generalist.
Magus isn’t “I can do everything.” It’s “I paid the price to learn every Element’s language.”

- Requires a set amount of levels in Vasilian. 
- You can learn manifolds from every element.
- You MUST learn manifolds from every element.
	- Ex: You must know at least one manifold in each Element before you can learn a second in any Element.

Although in lore humans can only learn one element if they have the ability, if a rare person is both Vasilian and Maxem; they are able to use their understanding of the fields to sort of "forcefully learn" the other elements. 


# The Vasilian

The observers of reality, philosophers of life, animal speakers.
Seeing the world from a different perspective and seeing what others can't has lead to a lot of legends on what their true capabilities really are. 
The skillset of the vasilian is unique only to the vasilian and the rarest of the beasts, the jinn.

## What a Vasilian Is

A Vasilian is not a maxem and doesn't evoke manifolds. They don’t bend the Fields to their will, and they aren’t a bard using music to sway a room.

A Vasilian is someone who can *read* the Fields—all of them—well enough that the world starts to feel… _louder_. Clearer. Less guesswork.
- **Maxems** shape _one_ Field. They perceive that Field the same way anyone perceives the world: through experience, senses, and practice.
- **Vasilians** don’t reshape anything. Instead, they can see how the Fields line up underneath reality, and they get better at noticing what’s “out of tune” as they grow.

In play, that means you’re at your best when you’re:
- spotting patterns other people miss,
- calling the right shots before things go bad,
- and turning small tells into big advantages.


You’re not omniscient. You’re just *really* hard to fool when you have time and focus.


## Design Intent

The Vasilian fills a role that isn’t martial power or Field manipulation.
- **Martials** force outcomes through skill and steel.
- **Maxems** force outcomes by manipulating a Field.
- **Alchemists** use intelligence to give them an edge.
- **Artificers** make sure their gear will outlast yours in combat.
- **Vasilians** use observation to perfect actions made.

This class is designed to:
- reward planning, scouting, and investigation,
- make teamwork stronger (you set other people up),
- stay useful in combat _and_ social play,
- and enhance any existing martial and aetherical capabilities.

A Vasilian rarely “wins” a fight alone, but parties with a Vasilian win fights more cleanly and safely.


## Seeing the Fields

The Fields are the foundation of everything; from a physical object, to the representation of a thought.

You don’t see glowing overlays or floating UI. You see the world like anyone else—except you pick up on the patterns that underlie the world:
- when someone’s words don’t match their intent,
- when an ally is about to overextend,
- when an enemy’s stance and breathing are “loading” a move,
- when the room itself feels _wrong_ in a way you can name.

As you gain mastery, you don’t gain “perfect truth.”  
You gain better signal: fewer false reads, more useful details, and clearer options.


## In Combat: Your Usual Loop

Most Vasilian turns revolve around four steps:
- **Focus (“Tune”)** on one or more Fields to be reading.
- **Mark** key creatures (enemies, allies, even the terrain).
- **Spend ("Exploit")** your setup (buffs, callouts, openings, pressure).
- **Do the rest** (attack, reposition, support, react).

Typically you Attune early, then keep your Marks/Threads where they matter as the fight changes.


## Out of Combat

Outside combat, you’re doing the same thing—just socially:

- reading intent and mood shifts,
- catching contradictions,
- choosing the right phrasing to land with someone,
- keeping an eye on who’s about to act first.

You’re the person who says: “Wait. That doesn’t add up.”


## How Vasilian Feat Trees Work

Vasilian progression is structured around layers of mastery: you don’t just get “more power,” you get more clarity, more coverage, or more ways to translate what you see into action.

Feats usually improve one or more of these:
- **Coverage**: how much you can track at once
- **Precision**: how reliable your reads are
- **Translation**: how well you turn reads into buffs/openings
- **Depth**: how detailed your analysis can get
- **Domains**: people, beasts, crowds, fields, negotiations, etc.


## Attribute Philosophy

Charisma has been called such for its popular use in social and animal taming contexts, but it is not charisma as the average person understands it.
Charisma here isn’t “being hot” or “being loud.” It’s the ability to connect to someone (or something) so cleanly that your words land like they were already true.

**Primary Attribute:** 
- **Charisma (CHA)**: How effectively you read the field (precision and depth)
**Secondary Attributes:**
- **Wits (WIT):** finding the best way to use your knowledge (translation)
- **Knowledge (KNO):** classification, signatures, lore (domains)
- **Presence (PRE):** mental stability and overload resistance (coverage)

**Attack vs Defense**: You roll Charisma vs a target’s Aetherical or Mental Defense, depending on the feature. 

**Key Skills**: (TODO) Aetherics (live reads), Aetherology (classification & lore), Perception (tells), Animal Handling (companions), Persuasion and Language x (social). 

**Resource**: Your limiter is Strain from Threads you sustain.


## Strain and Threads

You are not omniscient, you cannot track everything at once, you must choose where to focus.

**Threads**: A sustained focus or ongoing read you’re maintaining.
- Both Marks and Tuned fields can constitute a Thread. 
**Strain**: Your current Strain equals the number of Threads you’re holding. 
**Maximum Strain**: 2 + PRE. 

You maintain Thread as a way to keep tabs on creatures and how spread your concentration is.

~~Wits determines how many threads you can have on one creature, and how many of the fields you can be attuned to at once.~~ 


## Specialized Archetypes

Vasilian archetypes are the different areas of specialization and skill that vasilians tend to go into. 

You can mix them—most Vasilians do.

### Analyst

**Identity:** Tracker / weakness-finder.
You specialize in spotting exploitable patterns in specific targets; enemies, allies, and even yourself. 
Vasilians with this skillset are often hunters and trackers, able to be more useful against few targeted opponents.

Learn more knowledge about enemies.

### Companion

**Identity:** Beastmaster / field partner. 
Your plans include other actors—animals or bonded creatures that become part of your positioning and information network.
Vasilians who enjoy nature and deal with beasts a lot are almost always of this type.

Befriend beasts.

### Herald

**Identity:** Champion / Officer. 
Aiding allies, boosting morale, and focusing the enemy on you.
Many vasilians that work as soldiers and officers in war have taken up this identity and gave it its name.

Allies near you can benefit from your threads. 

### Speaker

**Identity:** Diplomat / negotiator. 
Social dominance and translation. You weaponize sincerity, intent, and timing.
Most rulers use Speakers as diplomats and to help them in treaty negotiations. 

You catch contradictions and “off” statements.



# The Martial

The soldier, the brute, the hunter. There are so many professions and skillsets that derive from the simple title of martial, that no one ever truly refers to one as simply martial. 
Possibly the most common skillset composes the martial, just about anyone can swing an axe or shoot a bow, which causes many to doubt the true skills of a martial.
But in expertise lies the true gap that make these so important to just about any group possible. You can have an army without a vasilian, but who would dare call it one without a knight. 

## What a Martial Is

A Martial wins by showing up prepared and being better trained than the person across from them.

Where some people lean on Fields, readings, or raw talent, a Martial leans on discipline and their own skill.

- **Vasilians** perfect choices by reading the Fields.
- **Maxems** change the world by shaping Fields.
- **Alchemists** use intelligence to give them an edge.
- **Artificers** make sure their gear will outlast yours in combat.
- **Martials** decide how the fight actually plays out.

In play, that means:
- you take and hold space (and enemies have to deal with you),
- you stay effective even when plans fall apart,
- the populace relates to you most,
- and you solve problems with gear, grit, and smart movement—not setup time.

You're not some baseline, you're the anchor and the reason plans succeed.


## Design Intent

Martials reward fundamentals: footwork, tool mastery, and discipline.

This role is built to:
- feel strong in _any_ terrain and _any_ kind of fight,
- reward knowing your kit and using it well,
- stay relevant outside combat through tactics, exploration, and leadership,
- react fast to situations, preventing enemy momentum.

A party without at least one Martial usually feels it.


## Attribute Philosophy


- **Primary (pick your axis or both):**
    - **Strength (STR)** for power, grapples, and breach.
    - **Dexterity (DEX)** for initiative, precision, and reaction time.
- **Secondary:**
    - **Stamina (STA)** to soak damage and increase defense.
    - **Wits (WIT)** to learn more depth of each weapon/armour.
    - **Knowledge (KNO)** to learn more weapons/armour.

**Attack vs Defense**: You roll Strength/Dexterity vs a target’s Physical Defense.

**Skills that matter:** (TODO) Tactics, Perception, Fortitude, Agility, Slyness, Riding, Survival, Crafting/Engineering.


## In Combat: Your Usual Loop

- **Movement** take space, close distance, pressure targets.
- **Force choices** feint, shove, trip, switch weapons, deny routes.
- **Exploit mistakes** riposte, disarm, hit openings, disrupt actions.
- **Defend Allies** block attacks, pull/carry to safety, create breathing room.

A lot of fights end up revolving around where you are and what you’re threatening.


## Out of Combat

You’re still useful when there isn’t a battle map:
- **Exploration**: tracking, concealment, route finding, hunting, concealment, survival.
- **Social**: command presence, respected.
- **Craft**: field repairs to weapons/armour, work odd jobs.

There's always something you can do


## How Martial Skill Trees Work

Your trees deepen one or more:

- **Weapon**: more weapons or more expertise.
- **Armour**: more armour or more expertise.
- **Maneuvers**: shove, trip, bind, disarm.
- **Mobility**: vaults, dives, climbs, mounts.
- ~~**Leadership**: callouts, noting enemy process.~~


## Weapons and Armour

Martials live and die by their gear—and by knowing them better than anyone else.

A rogue may specialize in light armours and thrown weapons. 
While a guard may take thrusting weapons and heavier armour.
Your kit is your identity, and your mastery is what makes it scary.

In Elester, adventuring and dangerous beasts are common, so weapon-smithing and armour-craft are extremely developed. Allowing for fine quality differences and upgrades to modify the same kit to work uniquely.


## Weapon Specialties (by Group)

Weapons aren’t just damage, each group carries a style of control and technique. 
Knowing a little of everything makes you flexible, but legends get made by people who master one path.

### Bladed (swords and knives)

Balanced weapons that are effective in slashing, stabbing, and parrying, making them reliable and adaptable in nearly any combat situation.

Critical Specialization Effect: The target of the attack gains [[Clumsy|Clumsy 1]] until the end of your next turn.

### Brawling (body and knuckle dusters)

Close-combat weapons that rely on brutality, grappling, or disabling strikes. 
These weapons enhance natural body attacks like punches and kicks or make use of martial arts techniques.

Critical Specialization Effect: The target of the attack gains [[d_Components/Maxem/Manifolds/Ice/Slowed|Slowed 1]] until the end of your next turn.

### Flexible (flails and whips)

Weapons that rely on **flexibility, unpredictability, and control**, excelling in tripping, binding, and bypassing defenses

Critical Specialization Effect: The target of the attack becomes [[Prone]].

### Impact (axes and poleaxes)

Weapons that deal *raw, concussive, or cleaving damage*, designed to break bones, crush armor, or hack through flesh with overwhelming force. 
These weapons rely on momentum, weight, and force rather than finesse.

Critical Specialization Effect: For each damage die of the weapon, add 2 additional damage.

### Ranged (bows and crossbows)

Weapons that strike from a distance with precision and force, requiring careful aim, reload time, or positioning for maximum effectiveness

Critical Specialization Effect: If the target of the attack is adjacent to a surface it gets stuck to that surface by the missile. The targets is [[Immobilized]] and must spend an Interact action to attempt a DC 10 Fortitude check to pull the missile free. It cannot move the its space until it succeeds. If the creature cannot become stuck, it gains [[Stunned|Stunned 1]] instead.

### Shields (as a weapon system)

Shields used offensively, either by slamming, bashing, or controlling an enemy’s movement with sheer force and weight.

Critical Specialization Effect: The target of the attack is moved up to 2 units away (you choose the distance), this is forced movement.

### Sweeping (glaives and quarterstaff)

Long-reach weapons designed for **wide sweeps, battlefield control, and overwhelming force**, often excelling at keeping enemies at bay

Critical Specialization Effect: The target of the attack becomes [[Off-Guard]] until the start of your next turn.

### Throwing (darts and atlatls)

Weapons meant to be **hurled or slung**, often lightweight or single-use but capable of devastating effects.

Critical Specialization Effect: The target of the attack gains [[Persistent Damage|1d6 Persistent Bleeding]].

### Thrusting (rapiers and spears)

Thrusting weapons that focus on precision, speed, and efficient stabbing techniques, making them excellent for aggressive and reactive combat.

Critical Specialization Effect: Choose one creature adjacent to the initial target and within reach. If its Physical Defence is lower than your attack roll result for the critical hit, you deal damage to that creature equal to the surplus in the hit to AC. This amount cannot allow for another [[Weapons#Critical Specialization Effect|Critical Specialization Effect]], and no bonuses or other additional dice apply to this damage.


## Armour Specialities (by Group)

Armour is how you trade speed, noise, and coverage for survival. 
Materials (cloth, leather, mail, scale, plate, composites) and upgrades (flexible, layered, rigid, stealthy, insulated, encumbering, water-resistant) matter.
Knowing what armour is most effective against an enemy can make a hard fight seem easy.

### Cloth

Often called the armour of the masses, it is the most frequently used armour for its simplicity and price

Armour Specialization Effect: Resistance 1 to all Cold Damage when using Medium Armour, this increases to 2 for Heavy or Advanced.

### Composite

Made from mixes of materials and techniques, the numerous overlapping pieces of armour provide strong support from piercing.

Armour Specialization Effect: Resistance 1 to all Piercing Damage when using Medium Armour, this increases to 2 for Heavy or Advanced.

### Leather

Solid boiled leather provides a strong protective hide not unlike the creatures it often comes from. 

Armour Specialization Effect: Resistance 1 to all Bludgeoning Damage when using Medium Armour, this increases to 2 for Heavy or Advanced.

### Steel

The strength of steel makes it ideal for those that need all the protection they can.

Armour Specialization Effect: Resistance 1 to all Slashing Damage when using Medium Armour, this increases to 2 for Heavy or Advanced.

### Wood

All natural armour that can be surprisingly effective, and dangerous up close.

Armour Specialization Effect: Upon taking a melee hit that makes the armour pass a breaking threshold, or if the hit was a critical hit: the foe takes 3 piercing damage with Medium armour, 5 for Advanced armour.



# The Alchemist

Alchemists are those who have set on understanding the world through their own means—mixing herbs, minerals, and strange extracts to create something useful… or dangerous.
Nearly every culture has its own tradition: apothecaries who make poultices, shamans who prepare ceremonial foods and drinks, and adventuring alchemists who turn all of that into fieldcraft.


## What an Alchemist Is

You’re a battlefield chemist and field apothecary. 
You brew Essences: toxins, caustics, poultices, stimulants, smokes, and travel provisions that can be used in whatever situation you deem fit. 

In the thick of it, you pressure space (dusts & vials), soften targets (coats & splash), and keep allies functional (menders & cleansers). 
Out of combat, you live in supply chains, foraging, identification, mixing, and adding a little spice to life.

In play, that means you’re at your best when you’re
- exploring around nature, finding unique materials to brew with,
- keeping allies functional,
- hold a stock large enough that any situation has a solution.

People’s opinions of you swing wildly: _fear of what you might carry_ and _gratitude when you cure what no one else can._


## Design Intent

The Alchemist fills a role of enhancement and support.
- **Martials** force outcomes with skill and steel. 
- **Vasilians** perfect choices by reading the Fields. 
- **Maxems** force outcomes by manipulating a Field.
- **Artificers** use intelligence to ensure their team has the best gear they can. 

This class is designed to: 
- feel useful in combat and downtime motivation, 
- ruin enemy plans by having an essence that solves the issue, 
- support allies from succumbing to weaknesses, 
- and enhance the capabilities of the party. 

A party with an Alchemist can adapt to a new situation faster.


## Attribute Philosophy


**Primary (pick your axis or both):**
- **KNO**: how many formulas you know, plant identification, difficulty of brews, and identifying effects.
- **WIT**: brewing and effectiveness.
**Secondary:**
- **WIL**: composure against mishaps and how many essences you can brew.
- **PRE**: refinement for aetherical brews. 
- ~~**CON**: Be able to invent new essences and finding aetherical reagents~~ 
	- (I'm unsure if this should just be regular Nature check but you can use CON, not really an attribute related to alchemist) 
	- (also idk how to rule inventing new essences, which should be KNO anyways)

**Attack vs Defense**: You roll WIT vs a target’s:
- **Physical**: for most things, bodily toxins, caustics, paralysis, corrosion.
- **Mental**: for other things like panic/flash/hallucination-style disruption.
- **Aetherical**: rare catalyst work that nibbles at field coherence (like anti-mana or pro-mana essences).

This is rolled when it takes effect for the first time.
- Thus, you don't know how likely it is to "take" if you poison some food or apply a salve until it is hitting the target.

Key skills: (TODO) Tool (Alchemy kit), Medicine, Nature, Perception, Persuasion.


## In Combat: Your Usual Loop

Often done in conjunction with other steps like your own attacks and movements, the more unique aspect loop of an alchemist is:
1. **Identify** the enemy essence (if needed),
2. **Draw** essence or ingredients (belt/bandolier helps),
3. **Quick-Brew** when you must,
4. **Deliver** (coat, splash, throw, feed).


## Out of Combat

Out of combat you live in **foraging, identification, mixing, planning, and supply chains**—and you’re never happier than when you have enough stock to answer any problem with a bottle.

Outside combat, you're the heart of the party while you prepare for the next encounter.
- **Gather** ingredients.
- **Learning** more effects.
- **Brewing** the next set of essences you think you will need.
- **Cooking** food that the party may not be able to access in most adventures.


## The Essence Crafting Model

You mix Reagents to create essences with different effects. 

### Finding Reagents

Once you knows the types of plants to gather and the parts of the animal to extract, you can begin finding reagents during exploration.
Likewise in downtime you can be shopping for those.

Different locations can have more access to different reagents, usually for named reagents too. 

Most reagent finding is quite abstracted to finding the core components as it often doesn't matter.
- Such as simply finding 2x Caustic and 1x Purifier after a successful exploration day. 
But some stronger and rarer reagents might need a specific ingredient, called a "named" reagent. 
- Like 1x Toxin (Dragon) as an ingredient.

### Reagent Families

The simple categories

**Harmers**
- Reagents that are meant to be used on the offensive
- Irritant, Caustic, Toxins
**Healers**
- Reagents that are meant to be used on the defensive or supportive roles
- Fortifiers, Menders, Purifier
**Modifiers**
- Ways to change the effects to be useful in different forms
- Solvent, Binder, Volatile, Aspirant
**Catalysts**
- Ways to form the different essences
- Ground Catalyst, Aetherical Catalyst

### Effect Reagents

**Irritants**
- Generally used to deter creatures and while painful and distracting, are not directly harmful.
- More makes effects last longer.
**Caustics** 
- Caustic elements make up most poisons and acids that are used with intent to damage life or gear.
- Rarer than irritants, they are generally more unique in effects and may need to be purchased/found from specific locations for specific effects.
**Toxins**
- Toxins are highly regulated materials, used when killing or destroying the target is of priority. 
- Often causing unintended consequences to any surrounding creatures and terrain

**Fortifiers**
- Ingredients that help soothe (not heal) the body from minor injuries, but are primarily used to enhance abilities 
**Menders**
- Exist to preserve life, ailing most diseases and stabilizing most wounds to allow for proper healing
**Stabilizers**
- The core ingredient to antidotes used to save people from succumbing to poisons

### Modifier Reagents

**Binder** 
- Makes the essence want to stick closer together
- Used for creating Coating essences
**Solvent**
- Ensures the essence stays liquid and fresh, also removes much of the scent
- Used for created Ingested essences
**Volatile**
- Increases the energy of the essence, letting it take effect faster and can turn the essence explosive
- Used for Vials
**Aspirant**
- Dries the essence into a stable powder so fine it seems to hold its place in the air
- Used for Inhaled essences

### Catalyst Reagents

**Ground Catalyst**
- The base compound that allows all other materials to mix properly into the desired effects
- Can use water in a pinch for weaker effects (a success is a diluted essence, and a critical success is a standard essence)
**Aethereal Catalyst**
- Effectively ground up crystals in a unique way
- Needed for stronger and more targeted effects, more effects

### Potency

Essence potency can be changed for stronger and weaker effects depending on how effectively it was created. 

**Potent**
- If you roll high enough, the essence is purer and the effects may be a little stronger
**Standard**
- The standard effect from a success
**Diluted**
- If you fail the roll, you may sometimes get a weaker version
- Or if you purposely dilute the essence with water.


## Brewing, Quality, and Mishaps

**Brew**
Roll when you have the required reagents and kit.  
	`Brew Check = d20 + (kit proficiency) + WIT + modifiers `

You can only brew up to `1 x WIL` essences a day (which can be increased later).

 **Quick-Brew** lets you brew an essence for 2 AP but the success tiers are shifted by 2:
 - Passed check (success) is a failure, Diluted
 - Failed check (failure) is a critical failure, Mishap

 A brew will always use the reagents and equipment. 

**Brew DC** 
	`Brew DC = 10 + 2 x Tier` 

The DC may be reduced by your Presence if it uses an Aetherical Catalyst.

| Result           | Amount      | Effects                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Critical Success | Brew DC + 7 | Potent Essence. The essence is purer and the effects may be a little stronger |
| Success          | Brew DC + 0 | Standard Essence. The essence brewed with no consequences.                    |
| Failure          | Brew DC - 5 | Diluted Essence. The essence is weaker.                                       |
| Critical Failure | Any Worse   | Mishap. Something went wrong during brewing.                                  |

## Essence Types

**Injury**
- Are applied to wounds or inflicted via an object like a dart
- Include salves, poultices, or are coated on weapons to penetrate the skin
**Contact (Doused)**
- Close range application that only needs to touch the skin
- Include salves, poultices, or liquids that are thrown on
**Shatter (Vial)**
- Are thrown or hit, breaking the glass and letting a reaction take place that is generally explosive
**Inhaled**
- Dust or smoke that stays in the air and takes some time to settle
- Gusts of wind can push it around
- Loses potency gradually once it has been released 
- Often cracked and spread around or burnt and released as it spreads
**Ingested (Potion)**
- Either drunk or eaten
- The most traditional and common type of Essence
**Provision**
- The art of essencecraft applied to meals and drinks in a subtle form for a truly wondrous experience


## Specialized Archetypes

**Toxicologist (Assassin)** 
- Injury essences applied to darts and Ingested essences applied to food
- Often using specialized caustics or more to hit individual targets
**Shaman (Chirgeon)** 
- Healers using a mix of essence types to heal the party whatever the situation
- Planners who prepare essences ahead of time for the given situation to ensure the party is at their best
**Grenadier** 
- Vials and Dust, throwing chaos and controlling space with toxic areas
- The widest use of Reagents to get unique effects 
**Antidoter**
- Specializes in knowing how to counter essences, both positive and negative ones
- Ingested and Contact. 
**Provisioner** 
- Meals & drinks, a small gesture goes a long way towards morale and optimal rest



# The Artificer

## What an Artificer Is

An Artificer is a creator, advancing what people can achieve without and even beyond that of the fields.  

An Artificer is someone who understands how things work, and how to ensure they stay that way.

- **Maxems** change the world by shaping Fields.
- **Martials** apply force through training and position.
- **Vasilians** perfect decisions through observation.
- **Artificers** make _everything else work better_ by design.

In play, that means you’re the person who:
- repairs gear so it doesn’t fail at the worst moment,
- modifies weapons and armour to fit the job at hand,
- changes the capabilities of your side. 
- and keeps the party effective across long campaigns and harsh environments.


## Design Intent

The Artificer fills a **structural role**, not a burst-power one.
Most adventuring artificers are usually smiths trying to earn some quick coin to open their own shop, or martials who only trust what they can make with their own hands. 

This archetype is designed to:
- keep the party going for longer,
- make equipment matter without becoming tedious about shopping,
- integrate tightly with existing weapon, armour, and upgrade systems,
- and help the party recover from any poor planning or misleading information.

An Artificer rarely steals the spotlight mid-combat.  
But parties without one feel the difference after three fights, false information, or a failed resupply.


## Understanding Tools, Not Fields

You don’t see glowing lines or hidden forces.  
You see **stress points**, **tolerances**, and **failure modes**.

Where others see a sword, you see:

- where it will chip,
- how the balance could be shifted,
- which coating will survive acid rain,
- and how many fights it has left before it needs work.

As you gain mastery, you don’t gain “perfect craftsmanship.”  
You gain **fewer surprises**.


## In Combat: Your Usual Loop

Most artificer moves are used sparingly during encounters, they revolve around preparation and reaction rather than raw offense:
- **Deploy**: prepared tools (traps),
- **React**: to equipment damage, hazards, or terrain shifts, (repair)

You’re strongest when the fight goes _almost_ wrong—and then doesn’t thanks to you.


## Out of Combat

Outside combat, this is where you shine:
- upgrading and preparing weapons and armour,
- repairing damage that would otherwise linger,
- maintaining other mechanical equipment for longer travel,
- negotiating with guilds, smiths, and engineers.


## How Artificer Feat Trees Work

Artificer progression focuses on **depth and breadth of application**, not raw numbers.

Feats usually improve one or more of these:

- **Access**: what tiers of upgrades, materials, and mods you can work with,
- **Efficiency**: time, cost, and material savings,
- **Adaptability**: swapping, refitting, or field-adjusting gear,
- **Reliability**: reducing risk, failure, or degradation,
- **Scope**: weapons, armour, shields, tools, siege, or infrastructure.

You don’t get “stronger items.”  
You get **more correct items**.


## Attribute Philosophy

Artificers are intentionally multi-attribute dependent.
**Primary Attribute:**
- **Knowledge (KNO):** understanding materials, systems, and designs
**Secondary Attributes:**
- **Wits (WIT):** improvisation, field fixes, creative solutions
- **Strength (STR)**: applying the upgrade or repair
- **Will (WIL)**: how many upgrades you can do in a day

**Attack vs Defense:**  
Most Artificer actions don’t roll to attack. When they use deployables, those items have their own stat blocks.
Repairs use Strength rolls towards specified DCs.

**Key Skills:** (TODO) Engineering, Crafting, Tool x, Aetherology (for interacting safely with aether-touched materials), Tactics, Perception.


## Resource Pressure

Your limiter isn’t mana or strain, it’s time, access, and materials.

You’re strongest when you're
- given preparation windows,
- know what to expect,
- and are supported by guild access or workshops.

Field work is possible, but never as clean as shop work.


## Specialized Archetypes

Most adventuring Artificers mix between specialities or go into an area that compliments their survival best.
Other Artificers may be generalists to support a town, or a specialist at a city.

### Armorer

**Identity:** Gear optimizer / front-line engineer.
You specialize in armour systems, layering, and protection.
- tailoring armour to specific damage types or environments,
- reinforcing weak points and joints,
- managing weight vs coverage tradeoffs,
- integrating shields and body coverage intelligently.

You make people harder to kill, without slowing them down more than necessary.

### Weaponsmith

**Identity:** Edge tuner / combat engineer.
You focus on weapons as systems.
- balance, grip, reach, and momentum tuning,
- specialized modifications for creature types or tactics,
- maintenance that keeps weapons lethal over long engagements,
- rapid field repairs and emergency refits.

It's hard to win a fight if you have a weak defense. It's impossible without an offence. 

### Trapper

**Identity:** Gadgeteer / problem solver.
You work with devices, traps, and mechanical solutions.
- deployables, braces, lifts, anchors, and supports,
- defensive structure building and room holding,
- non-aetherical counters to magical problems,
- infrastructure and siege work.

You solve problems that magic would be wasteful for.

### Quartermaster

**Identity:** Logistics officer / sustain specialist.
You manage supplies, durability, and readiness.
- optimizing loadouts for travel and combat,
- rationing, redundancy, and contingency planning,
- reducing upkeep costs for vehicles.

In long campaigns, you’re indispensable.
